## Recurring beliefs (quotes)
1. EVALUATION, NOT INTELLIGENCE, IS THE HARD PROBLEM. Ansari: "not like typical software where you can say yes or no to whether it works... how well does it work, and what are the thresholds to go into production?" Kaur: "You cannot optimize an engineering framework you aren't systematically measuring." / "Prompt Whack-a-Mole" / "80% accuracy ceiling". "If you do enough evaluations, you have essentially trained your own model." / "You are building a STRUCTURAL TAXONOMY OF HOW THE MODEL FAILS." (blog: why-the-ai-evaluation-layer-is-your-real-ip). Rumi Allbert: "we don't rely on vendor benchmarks... human expert consensus as ground truth."
2. DEMO vs PRODUCTION; Esposito: "a SYSTEMS PROBLEM MASQUERADING AS A MODEL PROBLEM" / "Stress Testing Is Not a One-Time Event" / "Benchmarks measure performance in isolation". Ansari+Qiao: "production environments expose failure modes that controlled tests never reveal" / "The bottleneck is... trust" / "evals serve as the guardrails for your inference".
3. HUMAN JUDGMENT IS THE FOUNDATIONAL MATERIAL, NOT POLISH. Esposito: "Human judgment is not a finishing layer... It is the foundational material" / "VERIFIABLE INTERMEDIATE STEPS, NOT END-TO-END AGREEMENT, are the practical unit of quality" / "quality control... cannot be reduced to agreement rates". Ansari: "The future of AI will not be artificial. It will be human judgment, amplified." / "A model reflects the weighted aggregate of the human judgment distilled into it."
4. TRUTH vs TASTE (Ansari, Forbes 2026-05-22): factual disagreement is "real but bounded" and converges; "For taste, that doesn't really exist... you will keep diverging."
5. EXPERTS MUST BE "BETTER THAN THE MODELS"; verifiers/rubrics are the product. "the most important ingredient of creating high-quality RL environments — and the most expensive — is the human data". Forum 2026-08-03: "RL environments (seeding data, tasks, verifiers, rollouts)"; "training is only 2% of the work." Bain: "people who are smarter than the models."
6. HUMANS-FIRST / experts not interchangeable. "We are in the talent business." "we should never call it annotation again." Expert Happiness Index drives PM comp.
7. MERITOCRACY over credentials; "just start doing the job... candidates who proactively demonstrate their ability to do the work stand out."
8. SAFETY AS COMPETENCE, NOT REFUSAL: "True safety is competence under constraint" / "Safety that depends on silence is fragile."
9. Mission register: "Achieving AGI is bottlenecked by... high-quality data"; "where should humanity spend its time?"; "I hope AI systems will be managed by humans, forever". Lowercase "micro1" always.

## Resonance levers
1. Lead with the FAILURE TAXONOMY, not the feature list (repro per mode; fixed vs accepted) — mirrors Cortex "where, why, when, how the agent fails".
2. Build your own verifier and SHOW ITS BLIND SPOTS (they sell verifiers; explicit on LLM-judge limits).
3. Trajectory as evidence; annotate where YOUR judgment overrode the agent: "the agent produced convincing code here; it was wrong because X; I caught it via Y" = the tagline enacted.
4. "Verifiable intermediate steps, not end-to-end agreement": requirement decision -> test -> evidence checkpoints.
5. Make requirements decisions explicit and reversible: DECISIONS.md with assumption, alternatives, TRADEOFF chosen.
6. Distinguish TRUTH from TASTE: prove the testable; declare the taste and pick briefly.
7. Reproducibility as respect: one command, pinned deps, deterministic tests, golden reference.
8. Meritocratic understated tone: the work is the résumé.

## Don'ts
No undisclosed agent output; no "AI did it"/autonomous framing; no single pass rate as quality; no "passed the LLM judge"; no over-engineered demo (boring stress-tested core); no "works in normal cases"; no annotation/crowd language; no credentials in README; no silent scope cuts — explain the boundary and the alternative ("competence under constraint").
